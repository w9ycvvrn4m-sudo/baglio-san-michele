JOINING

How one enters

A simple path, without haste. One does not buy a place; one approaches a house that already has its rhythm.

Riad Al-Uns is not yet a house open on a medina threshold. It is a precise project: applications, workshops and support are received from now, with a view to the opening.

That does not change the rule. One does not enter by volume or by urgency. The house is not a hotel, nor a school where one enrols online. It is a traditional dwelling and an intentional community of human scale: one lives there, one prays there, one works a craft, one shares the table and the silence.

The detailed arrangements — durations, the full rule of life, material conditions — are communicated after a first serious exchange, not by public form. → Frequently asked questions

THE PATH

Six gestures, without haste

The same order holds for a brief stay, a workshop or a residency. One reads first. One writes next. One does not force the door.

1

Read

Before writing, read enough to know where one believes one is entering. The project is not a tourist stay. Dar al-Hiraf is not an initiation workshop. Futuwwa is not a slogan.

Useful pages: the project, who we are looking for, the rhythm, futuwwa, the glossary.

2

Write

A clear message is enough. Who you are. What you are asking for — a stay, a workshop, a residency, or something else. What you have already understood of the house. No long file with the first sending: length will come, if it is needed, after the exchange.

→ Contact · info@al-uns.com

3

The exchange

The house replies. The exchange allows one to discern the agreement — or the absence of agreement — between a request and the rhythm of the place. It is neither a job interview nor a worldly selection: it is a measure of seriousness on both sides. A refusal is not an insult; an acceptance is not an acquired right.

4

Stay or workshop

Two distinct doors of entry, before any long residency:

  • • Staying — being in the house while respecting its rhythm, without a commitment of formation.
  • • Workshop — a few days or weeks beside a master, in a single chosen craft, under conditions. A real door toward a possible application, or a complete experience in itself.

5

The residency

The long commitment: to live under the same roof, to pray, to work, to share the table. Reserved for those of age. The rhythm is given — prayer, table, ateliers — ; this is not a surveillance of hours. One agrees on the gesture; one does not clock in. The rule of life is handed over and signed before settling in: the frame of the house, not an argument of communication.

→ The residency

6

The horizon: Al-Athar

One does not leave the residency without having left in the walls a work of one's hand, received by the master. This is not a ceremony added on: it is the formation arrived at its term. What then remains is not a title of former resident — it is a malaka and a useful trace.

→ Al-Athar · → After the residency

THREE DOORS

What one may ask for

Three approaches, one same threshold. From the first message one specifies which is being asked for — without confusing them. The guest of the Munya is another category: he does not enter by these three doors.

WITHOUT OTHER CONDITION

What one is, not what one buys

Young men who are poor or in a situation of precarity are received at the Riad with no other condition than their compatibility with the principles of futuwwa and of furusiyya. They may remain for a limited time, or for the duration — according to their capacity to enter the common life and their will to learn a craft.

At the Riad one is not judged on what one can buy, but on what one is — in act and in potency. It falls to the house to bring these aspects of personality and character into flower, including in the one who has not yet had the possibility.

ANOTHER HOUSE

The guest of the Munya

Al-Munya is the adjoining house: a distinct entrance, an autonomous kitchen, a majlis of closed number. One does not enter it by the path of the Riad. One is received there on presentation or by invitation — never by form, nor by the purchase of a place.

The guest of the Munya is neither the visitor of the Riad, nor the workshop student, nor the resident. An eventual passage toward the Riad — by the single door of the common wall, which opens from the Riad's side — remains a gesture of those who live there, under conditions set by the house. It is not a right of the invitation, nor a prerogative of money.

His formation is not that of craft, nor of the trades of Dar al-Hiraf. It bears on character and personality. There one forges — in the school of humility and of discipline — a man capable of holding a charge: a member of the Kingdom's governing class, not by displayed rank, but by service.

Even for a few days, and by periodic stays, one accompanies him on the path that shapes his person: the sense of community, the spirit of service, charity toward those who have not, loyalty, honour.

→ Al-Munya · al-munya.com · info@al-munya.com

What the house does not do

  • • It does not open a public form, nor a waiting list.
  • • It does not sell a place, a status, nor a “network of former residents”.
  • • It does not communicate the full rule of life before a serious exchange.
  • • It does not confuse the guest of the Riad, the workshop student, the resident and the guest of the Munya.
  • • It hurries no one: the rhythm of the house takes precedence over the urgency of the one who asks.

The motto holds for the entrance as well.

الرِّياضُ لِأَهْلِهِ، لَا أَهْلُهُ لِلرِّياضِ

ar-riyāḍu li-ahlihi, lā ahluhu li-r-riyāḍ

“The riad is for its own, not its own for the riad.”
