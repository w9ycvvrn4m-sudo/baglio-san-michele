DAR AL-HIRAF — BODY, WORD, SOUND

Choreutic discipline

Three gestures transmitted as a craft — the body, sung speech, rhythm — never a spectacle.

At Dar al-Hiraf the body is trained, the voice is trained, the ear is trained — not for display, but as disciplines that shape measure, presence and loyalty to a form received.

BODY

The trained gesture

Choreutic discipline here does not mean dance as entertainment. It means the ordered transmission of postures, steps and collective movements that educate the body to hold itself, to wait, to advance without disorder.

The same demand that furusiyya places on wrestling and archery is placed here on the group: no one moves alone; each holds the measure of the others.

WORD

Malhun — sung speech

Malhun is not a concert. It is a living repertoire of Maghrebi sung poetry, transmitted from master to disciple, where the word carries doctrine, story and measure of the breath.

At Dar al-Hiraf it is learned as a craft: listening first, then the line, then the full piece — never as a show for guests.

RHYTHM

Gnawa — transmission of the maalem

Gnawa, in the form received at the Riad, is not folklore. It is the transmission of a repertoire and a posture under a maalem — the master who holds the chain of the tradition.

Rhythm here trains the body as much as the ear: repetition, call and response, the long night of listening.

ONE FAMILY

With furusiyya and the crafts

Choreutic discipline stands beside the physical disciplines of furusiyya and the manual crafts of Dar al-Hiraf. Same logic: a form received, a master, years of repetition, no improvisation as identity.
