DAR AL-HIRAF

Hizam al-Itqan

The belt of mastery. Four grades, from undyed raw hide to gold thread. Nothing is gained by seniority alone.

حِزَامُ الإِتْقَانِ

ḥizām al-itqān — the belt of accomplished execution

Progression follows competence actually shown. A sequence of browns, from raw hide to very dark, marks a personal path without displaying it as a visible hierarchy. The sign is worn at the waist: it is not a title, it is a measure.

GRADE 1

Cru, undyed

The entrance. The novice receives the raw hide. Nothing is yet earned except having crossed the threshold and accepted the rhythm. The belt says: one begins.

GRADE 2

Brun clair

Advanced formation. The hand already holds a gesture. It is not yet mastery: it is the proof that the craft has begun to take root. This brown is not bought. It is shown.

GRADE 3

Brun foncé

Almost black: the proximity of mastery. The man holds the craft well enough to be trusted. He is not yet the one who transmits. He is already the one who no longer leaves the gesture to chance.

GRADE 4

Fil d'or

Dark brown, embroidered in gold thread: the master. The belt bears, in Andalusian Kufic, an authenticated hadith.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ إِذَا عَمِلَ أَحَدُكُمْ عَمَلًا أَنْ يُتْقِنَهُ

“Allah loves that when one of you does a work, he does it with excellence.”

WHAT IT IS NOT

Neither seniority nor costume

The Hizam is not a stripe. It is not worn to be seen from afar. The naib and the muqaddam, for the office, replace the hide with the mdama belt — a sign of charge, not of grade. The maalem, once the Al-Hulla is put on, no longer shows the belt: the whole garment takes up the sign.

The Ash-Shaykh alone wears the gold-thread embroidery at all times, outside ceremonies: his authority has no need of a scale. See Al-Kiswa.

THE CHAIN

Who holds, who transmits

The grade says what the hand holds. Governance says who answers. After the residency, what remains is not the belt: it is the malaka — and, one day, a household to hold.
