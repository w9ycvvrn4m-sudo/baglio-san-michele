DAR AL-HIRAF

Hizam al-Itqan

The belt of mastery. Three tones of leather, from beige to brown. Nothing is gained by seniority alone.

حِزَامُ الإِتْقَانِ

ḥizām al-itqān — the belt of accomplished execution

Progression follows competence actually shown. Three tones of leather, from beige to brown, mark a personal path without displaying it as a visible hierarchy. The buckle is plain, with no prong and no holes: one draws it tight, then knots it. The sign is worn at the waist — it is not a title, it is a measure.

FIRST TONE

Beige

Entering the workshop. The palest leather is handed over on the day a maalem receives a man into his shop. Nothing else has been earned yet: only that — having been received, and accepting the rhythm. The belt says: we begin. Before it, a man who has no workshop yet wears no leather at all: his waist is bare, or girt only with the cord of whatever charge he holds for the house.

SECOND TONE

Cognac

Advanced training. The hand already holds a gesture. This is not mastery yet: it is the proof that the craft has begun to take root. This brown is not bought. It is shown.

THIRD TONE

Brown

The nearness of mastery. The man holds the craft well enough to be relied upon. He is not yet the one who transmits; he is already the one who no longer leaves the gesture to chance. It is with this third leather that the private room comes.

OUTSIDE THE SCALE

The gold thread of the Ash-Shaykh

The fourth leather is not a grade: it is the Ash-Shaykh's, and his alone. Dark brown, embroidered in gold thread in Andalusian Kufic, it carries an authenticated hadith. His authority does not need a scale in order to be stated; and the maalem, for his part, no longer shows a belt at all: the Al-Hulla has taken the sign over.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ إِذَا عَمِلَ أَحَدُكُمْ عَمَلًا أَنْ يُتْقِنَهُ

“God loves that, when one of you does a work, he does it with excellence.”

WHAT IT IS NOT

Neither seniority nor costume

The Hizam is not a stripe. It is not worn to be read from across a room, and it says one thing only: the distance covered in the craft. What a man holds for the others is read elsewhere — on the jacket, the coat and the cord, in the colour of the charge, and only for as long as it lasts. The two signs do not replace one another: they are worn together. The maalem, for his part, once the Al-Hulla is on his shoulders, no longer shows the belt: the whole garment has taken the sign over.

The Ash-Shaykh alone wears the gold embroidery at all times, outside ceremonies: his authority does not need a scale. See Al-Kiswa.

THE CHAIN

Who holds, who transmits

The grade says what the hand holds. Governance says who answers. After the residency, what remains is not the belt: it is the malaka — and, one day, a household to hold.
