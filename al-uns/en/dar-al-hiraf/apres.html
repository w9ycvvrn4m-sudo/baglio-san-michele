DAR AL-HIRAF

After the residency

One does not leave Dar al-Hiraf as one leaves a school — and one does not leave empty-handed.

A man leaves here able to hold — or he has not left. The residency is not a parenthesis: it forms a man for what follows, a trade in the hands, a discipline of the day, a word that binds and, when the time comes, what it takes to hold a household.

What the house refuses is the worldly idea of an "afterwards": an address book, the status of a former resident, a say over those who remain. But refusing the network does not mean letting a man go with nothing but his hands. What he takes away has been prepared over years, and part of it is already in the walls.

What has settled in him

A trade held. Not a framed diploma: the ability to work alone, over time, outside the frame of the house. The master passed on a gesture; the man must be able to take it up outside, feed a table with it, and one day pass it on. Rank is little displayed and must be earned — Hizam al-Itqan.

A word. Futuwwa does not expire at the door. Loyalty, measure, refusal of vanity: what was required under the roof stays required outside.

What it takes to hold a household. Dar al-Hiraf is not a retreat. The man who becomes a maalem is ready, when the time comes, to marry, to honour his wife, to raise sons. The trade held, the discipline of the day and the given word are the same cloth as the honour of a house: a man who cannot serve in the workshop will not know how to serve a household.

What the house has set aside for him

A name in the walls. Every finished piece carries the formula ʿamal (عمل) — "work of" — the name of the hand that made it and the year; and, where an apprentice worked under a master, both names. This is not a keepsake: it is the only credential that counts in a trade of the hand. A client in the medina does not read a certificate — he goes to look at the work, and he can, because it stands in a building one can visit. See Al-Warsha and Al-Athar.

A house and a workshop. The work of those years on site has not vanished: it accumulated on a trade account, and that balance converts — if he wishes — into the first share of the house-workshop he will buy from the house, without interest, year by year. Often one of those the school itself raised in the derb. He has therefore already paid, with his own work, for a share of the house he will live in; and to the man who completes the whole path and settles in the medina, the house doubles the balance.

Tools. A master setting up needs irons before he needs advice. The chest of the futuwwa advances what is needed to equip the first workshop, without interest — a qarḍ ḥasan, repaid for what it is worth.

A chest that stays open. One remains a member of the ṣandūq after leaving, as long as the contribution is paid: illness, marriage, burial, the balance on the house extinguished if one dies too soon. It is the only bond that remains — and it grants no privilege, only relief between brothers.

What does not remain

No former resident acquires any right over the life of the Riad, over its workshops or over its admissions. One may remain bound by esteem and by the trade; one does not remain the owner of a place.

The house keeps no alumni network in the worldly sense. It keeps the memory of works and of the men who held — not a list of privileges.

The son of a maalem formed here does not enter by inheritance: he approaches like the others, by the same threshold.

Outside, the same rhythm

The ordinary horizon is a life in which trade, household and word hold together. Dar al-Hiraf prepares for it; it does not stand in its place. This was not a boarding school: outside, too, one owes nothing to an institution that would keep its men.

For some, a working link with one of the house's workshops may continue — out of the needs of the trade, not as a status. It is decided case by case, discreetly, never as a right acquired on leaving.

الرِّياضُ لِأَهْلِهِ، لَا أَهْلُهُ لِلرِّياضِ

ar-riyāḍu li-ahlihi, lā ahluhu li-r-riyāḍ

"The riad is for its own, not its own for the riad."

This holds for those who have left as well. The house owes them no career; they owe it for having received a frame and having honoured it. The rest — the name in the wall, the share of a house, the tools — is no favour: it is what they earned.
