DAR AL-HIRAF

After the residency

One does not leave Dar al-Hiraf as one leaves a school. One leaves capable of holding — or one has not left.

The residency is not a parenthesis. It forms a man for what follows: a craft of the hand, a discipline of the day, a word that binds, and — when the time comes — the capacity to hold a household.

What the house refuses is the idea of a worldly “after”: an address book, the status of a former resident, a right of regard over those who remain. One does not take the Riad away. One takes away what the gesture and the rhythm have deposited in oneself.

What remains is not a title. It is a malaka — a disposition acquired in the gesture and in the rhythm — and, in the walls, a work received. → Al-Athar

WHAT REMAINS

Four things, not a network

The ṣāniʿ enters in order to learn to hold. If he holds as far as the craft of maalem, what he takes away is not an affiliation: it is an aptitude — for the gesture, for the word, for the house.

A craft held

Not a diploma displayed: the capacity to work alone, over time, without the permanent frame of the house. The master has transmitted a gesture; the man must be able to take it up again outside the walls, to feed a table, and one day to transmit in his turn. Rank is little seen and is earned: Hizam al-Itqan →

A work in the house

The athar does not travel. It is set in place — a panel, a threshold, a basin, an inscription — and remains. Whoever leaves leaves a useful trace, not a personal souvenir. The house keeps the memory of the works, not a list of former residents. → Al-Athar

A word

Futuwwa does not expire at the door. Loyalty, measure, the refusal of vanity: what was required under the roof remains required outside. The man who has held his place at the atelier and at the table must be able to hold his word elsewhere. → Futuwwa

A household to hold

Dar al-Hiraf is not a retreat. The ṣāniʿ formed at the Riad, if he becomes a maalem, is ready — when the time comes — to take a wife, to love and honour her, to found a family, and to raise sons who may, if they wish and if the house receives them, come in their turn to form themselves under the same roof.

The craft held, the discipline of the day and the word given are the same cloth as the honour of the household. Who does not know how to serve at the atelier will not know how to serve a house. Who has not learned measure under a common roof will not have, outside, the constancy of a father.

The madrasa al-ula remains the family. The Riad prepares one to return there capable — it does not take its place. → Family & community

WHAT DOES NOT REMAIN

No right of a former resident

No “former resident” acquires a right over the life of the Riad, over its ateliers or over its admissions. One may remain bound by esteem and by the craft; one does not remain the owner of a place.

The house does not keep a network of former residents in the worldly sense. It keeps the memory of the works and of the men who have held — not a list of privileges. The son of a maalem formed here does not enter by inheritance: he approaches as the others do, by the same threshold.

WHAT FOLLOWS

Outside, the same rhythm

The ordinary horizon is the return to a life in which craft, household and word hold together. Dar al-Hiraf prepares for that; it does not take its place. It was not a college: outside, one owes nothing either to an institution that would keep its men.

For some, a working link with an atelier of the house may remain — by necessity of the craft, not by status. That link is decided case by case, in discretion, never as a right acquired on leaving.

The Riad is for its own so long as they hold their place there. Once they have left, they owe it to have received a frame and to have honoured it — not a career, not a tribune, not a right of regard.

الرِّياضُ لِأَهْلِهِ، لَا أَهْلُهُ لِلرِّياضِ

ar-riyāḍu li-ahlihi, lā ahluhu li-r-riyāḍ

“The riad is for its own, not its own for the riad.” This holds also for those who have left: the house does not owe them a career; they owe it to have received a frame, and to have honoured it.
