FURUSIYYA

الْعُدَّة

The tack

The bit, the saddle, the rule. What is placed on the horse figures what the house places around the man.

وَأَعِدُّوا لَهُم مَا اسْتَطَعْتُم مِّن قُوَّةٍ وَمِن رِبَاطِ الْخَيْلِ

“Prepare against them whatever you are able of force and of horses held in tether.”

Qur’an, al-Anfal 8:60

الْخَيْلُ مَعْقُودٌ فِي نَوَاصِيهَا الْخَيْرُ إِلَى يَوْمِ الْقِيَامَةِ

“Goodness is tied to the forelocks of horses until the Day of Resurrection.”

Hadith — Sahih al-Bukhari 2850

Furūsiyya gives the formation of character a body. Before it is a symbol, it is a practice: the art of the horse. The sani are made to hold an animal still untamed, so that they understand, in the gesture itself, what it is to command oneself.

The bit is no accident of the tack: it is the figure of the brake a man sets on what, in him, would carry him away. The passions are not bad in themselves. They become so by the use that is made of them. They are not extinguished: they are bitted — as one trains a colt, not to break it, but to turn its force.

The colt that rears and tries to unseat the rider is like the one who enters Dar al-Hiraf. The community provides the bit, the bridle, the spurs and the saddle. But it is he himself who must hold.

Each piece of the ʿudda has an office with the horse and a figure for character. The same logic runs through futuwwa, brotherhood and javanmardi: the house offers the frame; the man holds himself.

Al-ʿudda

العُدّة

al-ʿudda — the tack, the horse’s equipment

الحكمة

al-ḥakama

The bit

With the horse — speaks to the mouth
For character — the brake

العنان

al-ʿinān

The reins

With the horse — carry the hand
For character — intention

اللجام

al-lijām

The bridle

With the horse — holds the head
For character — orientation

السرج

as-sarj

The saddle

With the horse — gives the seat
For character — the rule

الجل

al-jull

The cloth

With the horse — covers and honours
For character — what is shown, without vanity

البطانة

al-biṭāna

The girth

With the horse — tightens without stifling
For character — measure

الركاب

ar-rikāb

The stirrups

With the horse — receive the weight
For character — the support of companions

المهماز

al-miḥmāz

The spurs

With the horse — awaken, without breaking
For character — demand

اللبب والحقب

al-labab wa-l-ḥaqab

Breastplate and crupper

With the horse — hold the saddle
For character — what the community places around

The community provides the bit, the bridle, the spurs and the saddle. It is the sani who must hold.

THE HEAD

The lijām

اللجام

al-lijām — the bridle

The bridle holds the brow. Without it the bit has no seat. First lesson: to receive a measure, not to lessen the force, but so that it can be led.

THE BRAKE

The bit

الحكمة

al-ḥakama — the bit

The bit does not take the fire from the horse: it gives it a language. So with the passions. They are not extinguished; they are bitted.

THE BACK

The saddle

السرج

as-sarj — the saddle

The saddle gives the seat. In the house, the rule holds the same office: it makes lasting possible. That is also, on a larger scale, the meaning of At-Tadbir — the governance that gives the house its seat.

WHAT IS SHOWN

The parade

العُدّة

al-ʿudda — the tack

Cloths and breastplate: what the horse carries outward. The ʿudda may be rich; it must never be hollow. What is shown honours, or it betrays.

THE RIDER

To hold oneself

الفارس

al-fāris — the rider

Authority is seen in the way a man holds himself beside the horse, not in the ornament. He who does not command himself does not command.

THE SĀʾIS

To hold and to serve

السائس

as-sāʾis — the groom

The sāʾis holds the horse. This is not servitude: it is the gesture that makes furūsiyya possible. He who has rank does not refuse the humble office — the same demand in futuwwa.

THE COLT

Who enters

المُهر

al-muhr — the colt

The colt that rears is not a defect: it is force before measure. The community offers the brake. It falls to him to hold.

This is the same young man the house is looking for: not the one already trained, but the one who has the force and accepts to be led. See Who we are looking for and The residency.

Furūsiyya forms the body; the ʿudda gives it its figure. The house does not train men in order to break them. It offers them a frame — bit, bridle, saddle — and asks them to hold to it.

“The riad is for its own, not its own for the riad.”
