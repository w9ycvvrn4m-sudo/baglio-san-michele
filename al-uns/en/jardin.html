RIYAD

Garden & kitchen garden

The garden is not decoration. It is a living, ordered and productive space, faithful to the tradition of the Arab riyad.

In Arab culture the garden is a model of order: water, shade, scent and food are thought together. The riyad does not oppose the beautiful and the useful — it holds them in one rhythm.

At the Riad three spaces are distinguished without being separated: the main garden, the secret garden (bustan) and the kitchen garden. Each has its function, its light, its silence.

This is not a pleasure park. It is a living system that forms the eye, feeds the table and recalls that beauty, here, begins with the earth.

STRUCTURE

The structure of the Riad

Axes, beds, basin, shaded walks. Water organises the path; trees fix time. One does not cross the garden as a backdrop: one enters it, walks it, learns to see slowly.

MAIN GARDEN

Courtyard and walks, citrus, myrtle, jasmine. Shade and scent for the daily life of the house.

SECRET GARDEN

Bustan more withdrawn: rare roses, lilies, chosen species. Denser silence, measured access.

KITCHEN GARDEN

Vegetables, herbs, fruit trees. Work on the land is part of formation.

ORIGIN

The chahar bagh — a diagram, not an ornament

The Islamic garden is not a vegetal décor: it is a cosmology set in the earth. And its origin is not Arab, but Persian.

The Greek word paradeisos — the root of our “paradise” — comes from Old Persian pairi-daeza (“circular enclosure”). From the Achaemenid period (Pasargadae, 6th century BCE), and especially under the Sassanids, the garden was already the image of the cosmos and of royal power: water, trees, geometry, closure toward the outside.

Islam does not erase this model: it absorbs and theologises it. The Quran evokes the gardens of paradise crossed by rivers; Persian architecture gives it a lasting form — the fourfold garden, the axial water, the wall that protects an ordered interior.

At Riad Al-Uns the garden remains this diagram: not a landscape to consume with the eye, but a place where order, water and work educate the body and the gaze.

WORK

Cultivating as formation

Residents take part in the care of the garden and kitchen garden. It is not an optional leisure: it is the same demand as in the craft workshop — regularity, attention, respect for a living material that does not obey haste.

What grows feeds the table of the house. What is pruned and watered teaches measure.
