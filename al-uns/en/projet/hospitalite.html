PROJECT

Selective hospitality

To welcome with measure. The stay is not a product: it is an entry into the rhythm of the house.

The hospitality of the Riad is selective by nature. It does not seek volume, but agreement: between the guest and the place, between the time of the stay and the rhythm of formation, of prayer, of the table.

مَنْ كَانَ يُؤْمِنُ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الآخِرِ فَلْيُكْرِمْ ضَيْفَهُ

« Whoever believes in God and the Last Day, let him honour his guest. »

Prophetic tradition — Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, nos. 6018–6019; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, nos. 47–48

Already in pre-Islamic Arabia, hospitality was not an option but an honour to be defended: the name of Ḥātim al-Ṭāʾī, the poet become a living proverb of generosity toward the guest, still carries its memory. The Riad invents nothing: it continues a duty older than itself.

Those who come are received as guests, not as clients. Selection protects the circle and guarantees the quality of the shared presence.

This hospitality is not a commercial service in disguise. It is the natural extension of the life of the house — open, with prudence, to those who know how to respect its tempo.

The gesture

At the door

غسل اليدين

The washing of the hands. Copper ewer and basin, linen cloth. The most frequent gesture, and the one most faithful to the tradition of the house.

A fuller welcome

Sometimes, for a traveller or a moment of particular honour, the washing of the feet. The same sobriety, the same intention.

At a significant entry — the arrival of a guest, the admission of a new resident, the passage from the Munya to the Riad — the washing of the hands is offered.

A muqaddam or the shaykh pours the water. The youngest of the house holds the linen cloth. The ewer, the basin and the small tray are the work of the chaser; the cloth, of the weaver; the light perfumes, of the perfumer.

This is not a formality. It is the moment when the guest is invited to leave outside the dust of the street and what has no place here. He enters an order in which generosity (sakhāʾ), fidelity (wafāʾ), courage (shajāʿa), sincerity (ṣidq), the mastery of the nafs and the quality of the fatā hold.

For one who stays several days, the house hands over the simple set (tunic, belt, sarouel, babouches, ṭāqiyah). The clothes of the street are washed, folded and returned in a cover.

The same honour is given to the distinguished guest and to the young man received without means.

Hands are held out. The name stays outside. The water removes the dust of the road before the house reads anything else.

Once the threshold is crossed, the guest is not a spectator. He takes part, when the house proposes it, in the same offices as the ṣāniʿ — the patio, the garden, the kitchen, the workshop — and sits at the common table. The intimacy of the house rests on that gesture.

Measure

Few guests at a time. A calendar that follows the rhythm of the Riad, not the reverse. Rarity is not a commercial device: it is the condition of presence.

Agreement

One does not welcome everyone. One welcomes those for whom the place has meaning — and who accept to enter into its order, without turning it into a décor.

Presence

The guest shares the table, the silence, sometimes a craft gesture. He buys nothing: he is admitted, for a time, into a house already living — sometimes even taking part in the Encounters that mark certain evenings.

Selection is made by correspondence and conversation. It concerns the disposition of the guest as much as the places available. The Riad reserves the right to refuse without detailed justification — not out of arrogance, but to protect what constitutes it.

For the practical modalities of the stay: Staying. For the order of the doors — stay, workshop, residency, and the guest of the Munya: How one enters. For the adjoining dwelling: Al-Munya.
