ADJOINING HOUSE

Al-Munya

A private house adjoining the Riad. Intended for a majlis of closed number — retreat, measured company, discreet hospitality.

Al-Munya is not a tourist annex of the Riad. It is a distinct house, with its own entrance, its own kitchen, its own circulations.

It allows what the Riad — a house of formation and of shared life — cannot offer: a space of regard, of silence and of measured reception, without confusing the two vocations.

A single shared wall. A door that opens only from the Riad side.

Futuwwa

What binds the two houses is not comfort. It is a rule of character.

Futuwwa — companionship, honour, service — forms the man first. It does not raise a man according to his means. It lowers what the world takes for a measure.

The young man received without means is raised: he is given a craft, a discipline, a place at the table. The man of means is received at that same table, in the same dress, at the same gesture. What is taken from him is the right to pay in order to be looked at.

One learns to deserve what he is given. The other learns to serve. Neither enters by what he has.

At their table, at a trial of archery, in the garden or in the kitchen, no one wears his origin on his face. The regard is the same.

The guest of the Munya acquires no right over the life of the Riad. A passage through it remains a gesture of those who live there — never a prerogative of money.

At the door

Outside, the world still reads the signs. The clothing, the luggage, the bearing.

One has nothing to show. The other arrives laden with signs.

The door receives them one after the other, in the same silence.

Outside, the world still reads the signs. The door does not read them.

In the house

Once admitted, the difference disappears.

One may be invited to a trial of archery or to a night dance. If there is work to be done in the kitchen or the garden, the guests are the first to learn.

The man who paid outside learns to carry water. The man who had nothing learns that he is looked at like the others.

Once the threshold is crossed, there is no longer the rich man and the poor man. There are only men under the same regard.

FEZ

The chain of the medina

Fez is not the setting of the two houses. It is the city where the chain of the crafts still has a name, a quarter, an amīn — and where that chain breaks faster than it is rebuilt.

Dar al-Hiraf holds one link of that chain: long enough for a malaka to be born. The Munya speaks the same material language. Its plasterwork, its wood, its cloth will come, in time, from the same shops.

To support the complex is to commission a gesture in Fez. The craft has a commissioner before it has a market. This holds for the plaster and the wood — and, when the time comes, for the saddle leather and the metal of the fittings, where furūsiyya meets the workshop.

The hours of the house

The rule of the two houses

Two houses, two entrances, two kitchens, two circulations. A single shared wall, with a door that opens only from the Riad side.

The Riad is intended to be constituted as ḥubus — or in whatever form the law of the Kingdom offers that comes closest. The property can no longer be sold or inherited; its fruits remain assigned to the formation. Restoration protects the walls. The ḥubus protects the vocation: the house will not be able to become a place of accommodation.

Nothing is conceived for accumulation or for display. Each space answers a precise use and a rule of measure.

Al-Munya does not exist to take the place of the Riad. It exists to allow, in discretion and in continuity, what the Riad, a house of formation, neither can nor should be.

THE RIAD'S THRESHOLD

Another path

The guest of the Munya does not enter the Riad by the same gesture. The Riad's path — read, write, the exchange, the training or the stay, the residency — remains distinct from the invitation to the adjoining house.
