GUEST HOUSE

Al-Munya

A private house adjoining the Riad. Intended for a closed majlis — retreat, measured company, discreet hospitality.

Al-Munya is not a tourist annex of the Riad. It is a distinct house, with its own entrance, its own kitchen, its own circulations.

It allows what the Riad — a house of formation and of shared life — cannot offer: a space of regard, of silence and of measured reception, without confusing the two vocations.

A single shared wall. A door that can be opened only from the Riad side.

Futuwwa

What joins the two houses is not comfort. It is a rule of character.

Futuwwa — companionship, honour, service — forms the man first. It does not raise according to census. It lowers what the world takes for a measure.

The young man received without means is raised: he is given a craft, a discipline, a place at table. The man of means is received at the same table, in the same dress, in the same gesture. He is denied the right to pay in order to be seen.

One learns to deserve what is given. The other learns to serve. Neither enters by what he owns.

At their table, at an archery trial, in the garden or in the kitchen, no one wears his origin on his face. The gaze is the same.

The guest of the Munya acquires no right over the life of the Riad. Any passage remains a gesture of those who live there — never a prerogative of money.

→ Futuwwa

At the door

Outside, the world still reads the signs. The dress, the bag, the posture.

One has nothing to show. The other arrives laden with signs.

The door receives them one after the other, in the same silence.

The door does not look at that.

In the house

Once admitted, the difference disappears.

One may be invited to an archery contest or to a night dance. If there is work in the kitchen or the garden, the guests are the first who must learn.

The one who paid outside learns to carry the water. The one who had nothing learns that he is looked at like the others.

Same dress. Same gesture. Same gaze.

The spaces

Sqifa

Threshold of welcome. Transition between the outside and the interior of the house.

Patio and hall

Heart of the house. A place of presence, of conversation and of shared silence.

Garden

A space of coolness and of regard. Continuous with the oratory.

Oratory

Looking onto the patio and the garden. Careful orientation.

Apartments

Guest rooms for a closed number. Simplicity and measure.

Apartment of honour

A reserved space, distinct from the ordinary guest rooms.

Salons

Places of company and of measured conversation.

Parlour

A discreet meeting space, apart from the main circulations.

Service

Dining hall

With a complete autonomous kitchen. Separation of kitchens from the Riad is total and non-negotiable.

Service rooms

Accessible only to the staff of the Munya. Distinct circulations.

Discreet store

A deposit for fabrics, pieces of value and objects kept. Few things, in their place.

The rule of the two houses

Two houses, two entrances, two kitchens, two circulations. A single shared wall, with a door that can be opened only from the Riad side.

Nothing is thought for accumulation or for display. Each space answers to a precise use and a rule of measure.

Al-Munya does not exist to take the place of the Riad. It exists to allow, in discretion and continuity, what the Riad, as a house of formation, neither can nor should be.
