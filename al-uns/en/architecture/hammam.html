SPACES

Hammam

Purification, heat, rest — a heritage that crosses the Mediterranean before reaching the Maghreb, and that remains, at the Riad, a place of the house on the same footing as the workshop or the prayer room.

HISTORY

A Mediterranean history

The hammam was not born in the Islamic world: the Islamic world inherited it. Its roots lie in Roman and Byzantine baths, which the early Muslim world adopted and transformed from the 8th century. In al-Andalus, from the end of that same century, the bath appears in residences and palaces before spreading through towns and countryside — carried by geographers who describe it, jurists who regulate its use, and physicians who praise its virtues.

مُتَفَرِّجُ الأَبْوابِ عَن صَحْنٍ ثَوى فيهِ الصَّباحُ وَشَرَّدَ الأَظلامُ

“Its doors open wide onto a courtyard where morning has settled and from which shadow has been put to flight.” — Ibn Darraj al-Qastalli, Andalusi poet (10th–11th c.), Wasf al-hammam

ROOMS

The rooms: a path, not a single space

The traditional hammam is not one room but a path through several rooms of rising, then falling, temperature — a principle found, under sometimes different names, from Andalusia to the Maghreb.

Room Arabic name Function
Changing roomal-maslakhOne undresses before entering the path.
Cold roomal-bayt al-baridThe body acclimatises on entry; one returns later to rest at length, over tea, once the bath is finished.
Warm roomal-bayt al-wastaniThe heart of the hammam: the largest, where most time is spent — light steam, massage, perfumes.
Hot roomal-bayt al-sukhunThe most intense: heat opens the pores and prepares the skin for black soap, then the kessa glove.
Furnacebayt al-narInvisible to bathers, behind the hot room: it feeds the whole system with heat.

This organisation in rooms of rising temperature is not an Islamic invention: it continues directly that of the Roman baths, whose correspondence of rooms the hammam inherited.

GESTURE

Wash, kessa, massage

Black soap, scrub with the kessa glove, rinse. When desired, traditional massage under an experienced tayeb — open to residents and to guests who request it, without being sold as a tourist product.

The gesture is transmitted as a craft: pressure, silence, respect for the body being worked.

IN THE HOUSE

A place of the Riad

The hammam belongs to the rhythm of the community. Guests may be admitted according to the calendar of the house — never the reverse. It is one of the clearest expressions of selective hospitality: one enters a form that already exists.
