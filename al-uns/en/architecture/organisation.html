THE HOUSE

Organisation of the spaces

Here, the order of the rooms

The house is not an assemblage of rooms. It is ordered according to precise uses: formation, body, knowledge, threshold, common life.

Each space has a function and a rule. If it begins to serve the image or the guest more than the resident, it must be corrected or removed.

NUCLEUS

The nucleus and the growth

The house does not open with every corporation. It opens with a single atelier — leather, iron and wood worked together, to the measure of a single ʿudda — and with the number of men that atelier can hold. The other crafts are added when the masters and the resources are there.

The plan provides for the possibility of growing — by a wing reserved from the start, or by the later acquisition of adjoining volumes on the slope opposite the Munya. The door that separates the two houses remains untouchable.

SLEEPING

The rooms on the gallery

One does not sleep in a corridor of equal rooms. The rooms open onto the gallery that runs around the courtyard on the upper floor, in the arrangement Fez has always given to houses where men work and lodge at once: that of the fondouk. Each room takes the measure the bay leaves it, so that none is identical to its neighbour.

The inequality of the rooms is not a fault of the building: it is what forbids putting a price on them. They are not distributed according to what one pays, but according to what the house judges — the work, the seniority, the charge held. The ṣāniʿ, the nāʾib and the maʿallem sleep under the same gallery.

No one sleeps alone. The residents lodge six beds to a room, with the men of the same workshop: the ṣāniʿ, the nāʾib and the maʿallem of one trade share the same room, so that the unit of life coincides with the unit of work. The house opens with four or five such rooms — twenty-five to thirty-five places — and may go, at full regime, to eight or ten. Single and double rooms do exist in the house, but they are at the Munya, for those received there. The upkeep of the room falls to the men who occupy it.

Young men of more modest means are received in the same rooms. Integration is part of the formation.

BODY

The zone of the body

Hammam, space of discipline (furūsiyya), changing rooms. Nearby: the water basin that crosses a courtyard — traditional form, not a swimming pool — and the secret garden of Damask roses.

On the slope opposite the Munya, a sheltered courtyard for archery and combat exercises. It is seen only from a private terrace or a passing oriel.

KNOWLEDGE

Knowledge

The room of the books and the small reading salon adjoining it. The khizāna — the cabinet of the volumes — remains under lock on the Riad's side. One asks for a book; the book is brought.

It is also the passage between the Munya, the apartment of honour and the Riad: the only room the three can reach when the door is opened from the inside.

COMMON LIFE

Common life

The single table, the oratory, the salons, the parlour. The patio and the garden with their fountains, salsabil and channels. Water is not an ornament: it structures the space. See also the natural coolness.

THRESHOLD

Government and threshold

The rooms of the Ash-Shaykh and of the Muqaddam are distinct — by function, not by comfort. A single door in the common wall with the Munya, openable only from the Riad's side. The key is held by the Bawwāb.

An inner Munya — an apartment opening onto the courtyard and the garden — is set aside for the direction or for an aged master. It is not a matter of comfort: it is so that a man who answers for the house may withdraw from it without leaving it.

An austere room of withdrawal, adjoining that of the porter, allows a temporary separation from common life. It does not humiliate: it restores the measure.

THE PATH

How one enters this house

The organisation of the spaces is not a plan to visit. It is the order of a life. One approaches it by the same gesture as for everything else: to read, to write, the exchange — never the inscription.
