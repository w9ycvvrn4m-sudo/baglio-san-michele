THE HOUSE

Al-Khizana

The locked deposit of the volumes, on the Riad's side. One does not browse there. One reads in the study rooms, in one's chamber, in the gardens or at the patio. Al-Khazin carries the book and takes it back.

خِزَانَة

khizāna — chest, cabinet, deposit of what is kept

In the Islamic tradition, the khizāna is not a reading room. It is a deposit: cabinets, volumes put away, a key. One reads elsewhere — in the study rooms, in one's chamber, in the gardens or at the patio. Access is not a stroll among the shelves. It is a gesture: to ask, to receive, to return.

THE PLACE

The deposit and the places of reading

The khizāna itself is the cabinet — or the cabinets — under lock. One does not remain there. The volume is opened in a study room, in one's chamber, in the gardens or at the patio. Books are not left spread out. They return to the deposit.

This knot is also the passage between the Munya, the apartment of honour and the Riad — the only room the three can reach when the door is opened from the inside. Knowledge, here, is a threshold as much as a deposit.

THE GESTURE

One asks for a book

Whoever wishes to read addresses Al-Khazin. The volume is carried to the place of reading. It is taken back. Nothing is borrowed “for later” without measure. One does not help oneself in the cabinets.

This is not mistrust. It is the same principle as for the atelier: the tool is not a personal good. It circulates under a rule, so that the gesture remains just.

THE CHARGE

Al-Khazin

الخَازِن

al-khāzin — the keeper of the deposit

A charge of the house, as the Bawwab holds the door. It goes to a resident who has no place in a workshop: it is a whole day’s work, not an addition to one.

The cut is everyone’s — serwal and jabador, cap, babouches. The colour is that of the charge: ink brown, after the iron-gall ink of the manuscripts. It sits next to the terracotta of the book workshop and the red of leather, because he keeps what the binder repairs. At the waist, not the leather belt that marks a workshop grade, but the mdama, the plaited cord, dyed in the same colour: it states a responsibility, and it is handed back with the charge.

He carries the book to the one who reads, takes it back, puts it away. He watches the state of the volumes, has them restored if need be, knows what is missing and what may enter.

Acquisitions are not a personal taste. They follow what the house judges worthy of being transmitted. Al-Khazin is not a librarian of an open hall. He is the passage between the cabinet and the reading.

WHAT IT HOLDS

What deserves to be kept

Treatises of craft, readings of adab, copies bound to furūsiyya, texts that form the character as much as the hand. Nothing that serves the news. Nothing that is consumed.

The khizāna is not an exhibition fund. It does not exist to be photographed. It exists so that a ṣāniʿ, one day, may hold a volume and find in it enough to hold his gesture.

IN THE ORDER OF THE HOUSE

A knowledge, not a décor

The organisation of the spaces places the khizāna at the knot of knowledge and threshold. It is not a “cultural space”. It is a room of formation, like the atelier, like the oratory, like the table.
