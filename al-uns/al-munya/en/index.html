Private house

AL-MUNYA

Adjoining the Riad Al-Uns wall to wall.
A closed majlis — retreat, measured company, discreet hospitality.

The name is not an ornament. In al-Andalus and the Maghreb, the munya was a villa of retreat: garden, water, shade, chosen company. A house away from the noise, and yet wholly a house.

Al-Munya is a private house, adjoining the Riad Al-Uns wall to wall.

It is not an extension of the house of formation.
It is not a hotel.
It is not an open place.

It is the seat of a closed majlis,
accessible only by co-optation.

The two houses have entirely separate entrances, kitchens, staff and circulations.
A single door in the common wall, openable exclusively from the Riad side.

Living in beauty, when it is not staged, already educates the eye and the body. The quality of the light, the weight of a cloth, the proportion of a patio, an old object that was not produced for you and that one cannot take away: all this precedes discourse.

The spaces

The house

Few spaces, generous, ordered. Each answers to a precise use. Nothing is thought for accumulation or for display.

One does not enter a munya as one enters a hall.
One is received, by degrees.

Threshold

  • Sqifa
    Entrance vestibule. He who crosses the door does not yet see. The intimacy of the house is guarded before it is offered.
  • Parlour
    First reception, distinct from any space of the Riad. Here one sits before being admitted further.

Heart

  • Patio with porticoes and hall
    The centre, and the motif of the name. Porticoed patio and major hall that opens directly onto it. Place of presence and shared silence.
  • Garden
    Water, shade, scents. Not a décor: the continuity of the patio toward the sky.
  • Oratory
    Oriented space of prayer, looking onto the patio and the garden.

Permanence

  • Guest apartments
    Private rooms with their own bathroom. Contained number.
  • Apartment of honour
    Unit of higher rank and greater generosity of space — the rank of the room, not of who occupies it.
  • Salons
    Closed spaces for restricted conversation.

Service

  • Dining room
    With complete autonomous kitchen, entirely separate from that of the Riad.
  • Service quarters
    Accessible only to the staff of the Munya.
  • Discreet store
    For valuable textiles, pieces in deposit and objects that must not remain on display.

Threshold

The rule

The number is closed.
Access is by co-optation alone.
What is said here remains here.

The rule precedes the guest.
It is not negotiated upon arrival.
One does not relax it to accommodate.

The guest is received.
He acquires no right over the life of the neighbouring house.
Crossing the common door, if it happens, remains a gesture of those who live there — never a prerogative of the guest.

Beside, in the Riad, it is the men of the house who look. The ṣāniʿ, the nāʾib, the maʿallem, the muqaddam, the shaykh: each, at his degree, holds the measure. They do not judge according to wealth. They judge the man.

At their table, during an archery trial, on a ride in the desert, no one wears his origin on his face. One does not know whether the one who sits beside him is a young man received by hospitality, or the son of a house that could afford the medina. The rule is the same. The gaze is the same.

One does not enter by what one has.
One is looked at for what one is.

Work

The furnishings

The Munya is not furnished once and for all by a decorator.
It is clothed, over time, by the work of the neighbouring house.

The motifs that will return on the walls and the textiles have nothing of an importation. They belong to a repertoire that has already made the journey — from the Islamic Mediterranean toward Venice — and that returns. Few pieces. Few colours. Matte surfaces. Nothing that has not already accomplished the going.

To support this house is to commission a gesture.
In the same city, by the same hand. A hanging, a light, a piece of woodwork.

The workshops of Dar al-Hiraf and the corporations of Fès will progressively supply textiles, stuccoes, ceramics, wood, metal and light. The craft thus has a commissioner before it has a market.

Before the eyes remain also, if any are found, a few old objects that were not produced for the guest and that he cannot acquire. They are neither collection nor museum. They are a model — and a warning.

What crosses the door between the two houses is not the men.
It is the work.
