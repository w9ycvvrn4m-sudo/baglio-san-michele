A house, and nothing else

Six rooms in a palace of the medina, against the wall of another house. Never more than eight men at a time. You do not get in by booking.

The name

In Umayyad al-Andalus, the munya was the walled estate to which the aristocracy of Córdoba withdrew from the city: irrigated gardens, running water, horses, poetry, very few people. Munyat al-Ruṣāfa, Munyat al-Rummāniyya. The word gave Spanish its almunia.

It is not a word from the hotel trade. It is the name of something that existed, and that was not open to the public.

What it is

A private house, restored according to the craft, made available to its members. Not a hotel, not a resort, not a place of treatment, not a spiritual retreat.

One comes for three nights at least and fifteen at most. The day takes the shape the five calls give it: no one is asked to pray, everyone is asked to be silent at those hours. After ʿishāʾ, the house does not speak again until fajr.

One table, one menu, the season. The hammam, the horse, the bow, walking in the hills. No screen anywhere in the house.

The telephone is not confiscated: it is offered that you leave it, and that is all. What is left is locked in a casket entrusted to a man of the neighbouring house, and returned to whoever asks, without comment, as many times as he asks. No one is obliged. The point is not to take the object away — only to make getting it back cost a gesture.

The house next door

On the other side of the wall lives a community of young men learning zellij, calligraphy, plasterwork, the forge, the horse and the bow. It does not belong to us. It would exist in exactly the same way if this house did not exist.

Between the two houses there is a single door. It opens from their side; on ours it has no handle.

It happens that a guest is received there — an hour at a master's bench, a meal at the common table, an evening of malḥūn. It appears on no list, is included in no price, and may be refused without a reason being given.

Entry to that house is not bought. At most, it is received.

What the house does not do

No alcohol.

No women received, no couples, no children.

No weddings, no seminars, no private events.

No press, no filming, no brand partnerships.

No online booking, no stars, no rankings.

No name published — neither of members, nor of neighbours.

The circle

It has a name: An-Nudama الندماء — the companions. It was once the name of the men admitted to the sovereign's private company in the evening: what was expected of them was bearing, few words, and to keep what they had heard.

One hundred and twenty, and not one more. Men only.

One does not put oneself forward: one is presented. Two members present a candidate; he comes once, for a conversation and a meal at the common table of the house next door. The following day the house asks the table. It decides, and gives no reason for its decision.

Names are written nowhere but by hand, in a single register kept in the neighbours' house. No digital trace of them exists, and none will.

There is no form on this page, and there will not be one.

What the subscription supports

A fixed share of the house's result is assigned by statute to the training bursaries of the house next door. It is not left to the judgement of whoever runs it tomorrow.

Two stays of seven nights keep an apprentice for a whole year.

The member knows which craft he supports. He will never know the apprentice's name: the register of the house next door is not public and will not become so.

Once a year he receives a handwritten line saying what was done — a panel laid, a door finished, a master kept.

The zellij, the plaster and the wood of this house
were made by the hands next door.

If someone has spoken to you of this house, he knows where to write.
