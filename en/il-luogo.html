THE FARMHOUSE

The place

A 16th-century fortified farmhouse in the Ragusa countryside: enclosed courtyard, stone wings, chapel, entrance hall and three rural buildings on the estate.

The southern front, toward the garden and the south portal.

The Iblean baglio is an organism closed toward the countryside and open toward itself. Thick limestone walls, a portal that filters entry, a courtyard onto which the rooms of work and dwelling look, a chapel, an entrance hall, the roofs from which the land is seen: this is the form the sixteenth century left in the Ragusa area, and which the restoration of Baglio San Michele intends to make habitable again without overturning it.

The drawing published on this page is the document from which the space is read. Ground floor and first floor, South, West and North wings still standing, a portion of the East wing to be rebuilt from scratch, chapel, workshops, entrance halls, garden to the south and three rural buildings outside the enclosure. The final figures for the units will come from Phase 0 of design; the geometry of the place is already this one.

How to read the drawing

Ground floor

The courtyard occupies the centre. The compass on the drawing points downward: on the sheet south is at the top, north at the bottom, east to the left, west to the right. At the top, the South Wing with the southern entrance hall (portal onto the fields) and apartments 11–15. To the right, the West Wing with apartments 4–10. At the bottom, the North Wing with the northern entrance hall (portal onto the outer garden) and apartments 1–3. To the left, the East Wing: workshop, laboratories and chapter house facing the courtyard; apartments 16–21 to be rebuilt from scratch. The chapel, with sacristy, stands at the north-east corner.

Outside the enclosure, Building 1 (135 m², of which about 60 existing) is the house facing the south portal. Building 2 (85 m²) is a restoration in Phase 2; Building 3 (175 m²) a reconstruction in Phase 2.

First floor

The first-floor drawing is the central body of the North Wing — the Tower — with anteroom, sitting room, library, bedrooms. To the east, the new rooms to be rebuilt from scratch. Terrace, verandah loggia and internal stairs complete the crown.

The vertical path of entrance hall–stairs–terrace is the baglio’s air chimney: air enters, rises and leaves through the roofs.

The courtyard

At the centre of the baglio there is no decorative void. There is a climatic and social device: the courtyard protects from sun and wind, collects water, distributes access, and holds together those who inhabit the wings. The ground-level basin, heir to the Mediterranean fisqiya, and the floor channels cool by evaporation; around them, the façades remain in shade for much of the day.

The wings

A complete baglio closes the courtyard on four sides. Three wings still stand — South, West and North. Of the East Wing the body facing the courtyard remains (workshop, laboratories, chapter house, chapel); apartments 16–21, on the eastern front, are to be rebuilt from scratch. The South Wing looks onto the garden and carries the southern entrance hall, with the portal onto the fields. The West Wing is the western front, already numbered on the drawing (apartments 4–10). The North Wing closes the enclosure toward the north, with the entrance hall onto the outer garden.

Entrance halls, chapel, workshops, garden

Beyond the dwellings the baglio holds the spaces that make it a collective house and not a rural condominium. The entrance halls are the thresholds: to the south the portal onto the fields, to the north the portal onto the outer garden. Whoever enters leaves the countryside and arrives in the courtyard. The chapel, at the north-east corner, is the sacred space that every Iblean baglio reserved within the enclosure. The workshops, on the East Wing toward the courtyard, return to being workshops. The garden to the south, beyond the portal, is the agricultural anteroom of the enclosure, not an ornamental park.

Water, stone, air

The restoration does not add a visible air-conditioning plant. It takes up the devices of the Iblean baglio and of the Mediterranean tradition: thermal mass of the walls, pergolas, vertical air paths, water in the courtyard. Water cools, stone accumulates, air rises. The genealogy of these gestures is written at length in Historical Roots; the energy picture, in Energy Autonomy.

The three rural buildings

On the lands of the farmhouse, outside the historic enclosure, the drawing places three bodies in Iblean stone. They are not a village of twenty lots: they are three buildings, sized on the drawing. Building 1, 135 m² of which about 60 existing, is the house facing the south portal. Building 2, 85 m², is a restoration in Phase 2. Building 3, 175 m², a reconstruction in Phase 2. They belong to Phase 2, together with the portion of the East Wing to be rebuilt. Families who have lived in an apartment of the baglio may, if the household grows, move to one of these bodies according to the transition mechanism defined by the Chapter of the Savi.

Two phases, one organism

PHASE 1

The historic baglio

Philological restoration of the South, West and North wings, of the existing portion of the East Wing, of the entrance halls, the chapel, the workshops and the courtyard. Fourteen residential units: apartments for families and cells for young singles in formation. Natural climate control, local materials, no volumes added onto the existing enclosure.

PHASE 2

Portion of the East Wing and rural buildings

Reconstruction from scratch of the portion of the East Wing (apartments 16–21) and construction of Buildings 1, 2 and 3 in Iblean stone on the lands of the farmhouse. The courtyard closes again on four sides; the three rural bodies remain outside the enclosure, sized for a larger household.

Times, costs and internal partitions are on the page The Project. The genealogy of the typology is in Historical Roots. The relation with the Ragusa area is in Territory.

Whoever comes to live here does not buy a view. They enter a stone enclosure that already has a form, a courtyard, a chapel and a relation with the lands around it. The drawing serves this: to show the place before the discourse on the project.
