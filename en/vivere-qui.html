DAILY LIFE

Living at Baglio San Michele

Life at Baglio San Michele follows a rhythm marked by the times of the land and the care of relationships. It is not a life of constant communal living, but a coexistence in which everyone can choose when to participate and when to withdraw into their own home. The families who choose the Baglio are generally people who appreciate the relational dimension without giving up their autonomy.

The rhythm of daily life

Days often begin early, with the sounds of the countryside and the aroma of bread or coffee prepared at home. Many families work remotely or dedicate themselves to artisanal and agricultural activities, alternating moments of individual commitment with time spent with children and community life.

Meals play a central role. They are not only moments of nourishment but of encounter. Often people eat together in the courtyard or common areas, especially on weekends and summer evenings. Products from the vegetable garden, olive grove, and neighboring farms naturally enter the kitchen of each family.

Education and raising children

Children grow up in an environment where nature, intergenerational relationships, and contact with manual work are part of daily life. There is no structured internal school, but we favor an educational approach that integrates formal learning with direct experience: the vegetable garden, caring for animals, participating in festivals and seasonal work, and contact with local artisans and farmers.

Families support each other in organizing activities for children and in mutual help during busier periods. The presence of elderly people and adults with different trades offers children a rich context of stimuli and role models.

Work and productive activities

Many residents work remotely, taking advantage of the available connectivity and dedicated spaces for study and individual work. Others dedicate themselves to activities related to the land, crafts, or processing of local products.

The Baglio encourages the creation of small productive activities internally or in collaboration with local businesses (beekeeping, horticulture, food processing, crafts). The goal is not profit maximization, but the creation of a dignified mixed economy consistent with the rhythms of community life.

Community life and mutual support

Each family has its own private home, designed to guarantee intimacy and tranquility. At the same time, the Baglio offers shared spaces — such as the courtyard, common kitchen, chapel, and green areas — that become places of natural encounter.

The care of common spaces is everyone’s responsibility. There is no “service staff”: the families themselves, according to rotations or agreements, take care of ordinary maintenance, the preparation of some events, and the daily management of shared environments. This model strengthens the sense of shared responsibility and reduces management costs.

Balance between privacy and community

One of the most delicate aspects of life at the Baglio is finding the right balance between the need for family intimacy and the community dimension. It is not a life of constant communal living, but a coexistence in which everyone can choose when to participate and when to retreat into their own home.

Respect for others’ time and space is considered a fundamental value. The families who choose the Baglio are generally people who appreciate relational life without renouncing their autonomy.
